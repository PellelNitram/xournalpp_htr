    WordDetectorOnline model

    Stroke-based word detector. It works on the pen strokes of a page instead of a rendering of it: a small gradient boosted model scores each pair of consecutive strokes as "same word" or not, and the stroke sequence is cut into words where the score falls below a threshold. It performs detection only; a recognizer (SimpleHTR or SimpleHTR-OnDB) reads the words afterwards. Integrated into Xournal++ HTR according to the ADRs (in particular ADR 006), with one exception: the artifact is a numpy file, not ONNX (see below). Tracked in issue

    168.

    The source lives under xournalpp_htr/training/word_detector_online/.

    Structure (ADR 006)

    File Purpose Deps
    strokes.py StrokePage, the strokes of a page plus word labels base
    features.py Spatial features of consecutive strokes base
    model.py Boosted trees as plain arrays, numpy evaluation, cutting into words base
    detectors.py The boosted detector and the tuning of its merge threshold base
    iam_ondb.py IAM-OnDB forms as training pages, with the gaps between words shrunk training-simple-htr-ondb
    data.py The benchmark documents as labelled stroke pages base
    metrics.py, evaluate.py Exact and box-coverage scoring, evaluation CLI base
    export.py Trains on IAM-OnDB, writes model.npz + config.json, HF Hub upload training-simple-htr-ondb
    model_card.md Published as the HF repo's README.md none

    The HF-Hub-backed inference class lives in xournalpp_htr/inference_models.py as OnlineWordDetectorModel. Deviations from the model conventions: there is no ONNX file (the trees are stored as plain arrays and evaluated in numpy, so inference needs neither onnxruntime nor scikit-learn), no separate checkpoint (training takes minutes, export.py trains and exports in one go), no Hydra config and no Gradio demo yet.

    Training setup (step-by-step)

    No GPU is needed. Training takes minutes on a CPU, most of it spent reading IAM-OnDB (not timed separately).

    1. Clone and install the base package (bash INSTALL_LINUX.sh), then uv sync --extra training-simple-htr-ondb for the IAM-OnDB loader.
    2. Authenticate with HuggingFace (hf auth login): the IAM-OnDB dataset repo is private.
    3. Check the install with make tests-not-slow.
    4. Score a detector on the benchmark, leaving the training data out of it:
    uv run python -m xournalpp_htr.training.word_detector_online.evaluate

    It trains on IAM-OnDB pages and scores every benchmark document. --iam-seed changes the draw of IAM-OnDB forms and --gap-squeeze LOW HIGH the shrinking of the gaps between words. 5. Export:

    uv run python -m xournalpp_htr.training.word_detector_online.export \
    --output-dir exports/word_detector_online
    1. Validate: the test tests/test_word_detector_online_model.py checks that the numpy evaluation reproduces scikit-learn's probabilities, and benchmarking the pipeline that loads the model must give the numbers under Best model.
    2. Upload with export.py --upload once validated.

    Inference

    from xournalpp_htr.inference_models import OnlineWordDetectorModel

model = OnlineWordDetectorModel.from_pretrained()
words = model.group_strokes(strokes)  # [(x, y), ...] -> [frozenset({0, 1}), ...]

    strokes is a list of (x, y) arrays in writing order and document units. The benchmark pipeline 2026-11-03_online_word_detector_hub_simple_htr_ondb uses the published model, renders each word from its own strokes and reads it with SimpleHTR-OnDB.

    Best model

    Detector trained on 700 IAM-OnDB forms stitched into pages with the gaps between words shrunk by a factor of 0.3 to 1.0, merge threshold tuned on out-of-fold probabilities, no post-processing. Benchmark pipeline 2026-11-03_online_word_detector_hub_simple_htr_ondb (SimpleHTR-OnDB reading crops rendered from each word's strokes): precision 84.1%, recall 87.4%, CER 27.3% (case-insensitive), recall × (1 − CER) 63.6%. YOLO with SimpleHTR scores 77.0%, 72.4%, 27.5% and 52.5% on the same 406 words; a paired bootstrap puts the online pipeline's lead in the combined score at +11 points (95% interval [+4.5, +15.9] resampling documents), driven by recall.

    Experiments

    The pipelines and detectors of the experiments below, and the code only they used, were removed because users cannot run them: they refit a detector on every run or need the private IAM-OnDB dataset. The experiments stay documented here; the code is in the git history up to commit 1e4bcb2, for example git show 1e4bcb2:xournalpp_htr/training/word_detector_online/postprocess.py. The pipeline names below are the ones that existed then.

    All numbers are on the benchmark of 7 documents by 7 writers (406 words) with the benchmark's box-coverage matching. The benchmark is never part of the training data of the IAM-OnDB detectors; the benchmark-trained detectors are scored leave-one-document-out, which also holds out the writer.

    2026-11-02 — Stability and post-processing

    • Hypothesis: the squeeze range and the draw of IAM-OnDB forms hardly matter, and merging fragments and dropping underlines improves the combined score.
    • Setup: detection scores of the IAM-trained detector for squeeze ranges 0.5–1.0, 0.2–1.0, 0.3–1.3 and 0.5–1.3, for five seeds of the form draw, and with post-processing tuned on 300 other IAM-OnDB forms (postprocess.py).
    • Results: all squeezed ranges give detection F1 0.85–0.86 (unsqueezed 0.84). Five form draws give F1 0.86 each (precision 85–88%, recall 85–87%). Post-processing raises precision from 84.1% to 87.1% and lowers recall from 87.4% to 86.2%, so the combined score falls from 63.6% to 62.8%.
    • Conclusion: keep the 0.3–1.0 squeeze and no post-processing; the detector is stable across draws of the training forms.

    2026-11-02 — Squeezing the gaps between words

    • Hypothesis: the detector merges neighbouring words because IAM-OnDB word gaps are wider than the benchmark's.
    • Setup: per IAM-OnDB line, the gaps between words shrink by a random factor between 0.3 and 1.0 (iam_ondb.py).
    • Results: recall 81.5% → 87.4%, precision 88.5% → 84.1%, CER 31.9% → 27.3%, recall × (1 − CER) 55.5% → 63.6%.
    • Conclusion: adopted. Remaining errors: hard handwriting, fragmented words and stray strokes (underlines, arrows) attached to words.

    2026-11-01 — Crops rendered from each word's own strokes

    • Hypothesis: crops cut from the page render contain neighbouring words, which hurts recognition.
    • Setup: each crop is rendered from the strokes of its word alone.
    • Results: with SimpleHTR-OnDB CER 36.6% → 31.9% and recall × (1 − CER) 51.7% → 55.5%; with SimpleHTR 38.4% → 34.2%.
    • Conclusion: adopted; this is an advantage over the vision-based detectors.

    2026-11-01 — Training on IAM-OnDB pages

    • Hypothesis: IAM-OnDB, with many writers, can replace the benchmark documents as training data.
    • Setup: lines of a form are combined at their original positions into pages (line breaks included); words come from gap cutting (simple_htr_ondb/word_splitting.py); optional synthetic underlines, arrows, boxes and connector curves.
    • Results: single IAM lines: detection F1 0.65; stitched pages: 0.84; synthetic non-text strokes: 0.83, no gain. Reference, benchmark-trained: 0.77.
    • Conclusion: stitched pages are what matters. The word labels follow a gap rule and only lines where it works cleanly are kept (about 1,800 of 5,000 examined lines were rejected, mostly as ambiguous gaps), which is a known limitation.

    2026-10-30 — Detectors trained on the benchmark

    • Hypothesis: an algorithm on the strokes can find words as well as the vision-based detectors.
    • Setup: gap threshold, single-linkage clustering and boosting on features of consecutive strokes, leave-one-document-out on the benchmark.
    • Results: detection F1 (box coverage) 0.77 for the gap threshold, 0.65 for clustering and 0.76 for boosting with threshold 0.5, 0.77 with the threshold tuned on held-out documents; the tuned boosting pipeline reaches recall 81.3% at precision 73.8%.
    • Conclusion: boosting works best; the benchmark alone is too small to train on honestly, which led to IAM-OnDB.

    Current status

    • OnlineWordDetectorModel loads model.npz + config.json and groups strokes.
    • One benchmark pipeline, 2026-11-03_online_word_detector_hub_simple_htr_ondb, uses the published detector; its numbers are under Best model.
    • Known issues: in a sample of the wrong crops about a quarter were merged words, and split words mostly show up as spurious boxes; strokes of arrows and underlines next to words become spurious boxes or steal a word's match; recognition errors on hard handwriting are not addressed here.
    • The benchmark is small, so differences of a few points are not reliable.
    • Missing compared with the model conventions: Gradio demo, Hydra config.

    Outlook

    • Better recognition of clean but misread crops (fine-tune the recognizer on stroke renders with realistic grouping noise).
    • Merge fragments and drop non-word strokes with a learned model instead of rules.
    • Word labels for IAM-OnDB that do not depend on a gap rule.
    • A Gradio demo to inspect the groups visually.
