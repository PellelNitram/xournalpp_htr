    Indra

    Datalab's pretrained Indra OCR (weights), tried as a page reader (issue #166). It finds the text lines of a page and reads them, run as ONNX through the indra-ocr package: no PyTorch, no GPU, no server.

    Optional. It is built for printed documents; handwriting is a stated weakness. Datalab reports 25.2% on its OmniParseBench handwriting tests and "about a third of the words on held-out handwritten pages" wrong. On our benchmark it is nevertheless competitive as a whole-page reader, but it does not beat the pipeline of record.

    Exception to ADR 006: the weights are not our own export; indra-ocr downloads them from the HF Hub on first use (about 76 MB for the default set, of which the Latin path is about 25 MB). The dependency sits behind the optional indra extra.

    License: the code is Apache-2.0, the weights are under a modified OpenRAIL-M: free for research, personal use and startups under $20M in funding or revenue, and not to be used competitively with Datalab's API. Check it before any commercial use.

    One pipeline:

    Pipeline What it does
    2026-11-21_indra_page The whole page through Indra's own line detector and recogniser. Lines are cut into words at the recogniser's spaces. No YOLO detector, no stroke grouping.

    How words are cut

    Indra returns lines, not words. Its recogniser (a CTC model) emits a token sequence per line, including space tokens, each with the x position of its CTC peak. IndraModel.read_page cuts each line at the spaces. A word spans from its first to its last token, and its box is stretched to the midpoint of the gap to each neighbouring word (the ends of the line for the first and last word). It uses the package's engine (detect, recognize), not its public Converter.ocr, which returns lines only; the extra therefore pins the version.

    GPU training setup

    Not applicable: we have not trained or finetuned Indra.

    Inference

    uv sync --extra indra

    uv sync removes extras that you don't name, so list every extra you use. To add Indra to a running environment, use uv run --extra indra ....

    from xournalpp_htr.inference_models import IndraModel

model = IndraModel.from_pretrained()
words = model.read_page(page_image_grayscale)  # [(text, BoundingBox), ...]

    Benchmark:

    uv run --extra indra python scripts/run_benchmark.py -p 2026-11-21_indra_page

    Best model

    2026-11-21_indra_page is the only Indra pipeline. Its R×(1-CER) is 57.5%, second to 2026-11-03_online_word_detector_hub_simple_htr_ondb (63.6%) among the pipelines that need no server, and below 2026-11-09_yolo_detector_lfm3_vl (58.4%), which needs a llama-server and about 20 minutes per benchmark run on CPU. A run of Indra takes 1 to 2 minutes. See Experiments.

    Experiments

    All results use the benchmark dataset latest as of 2026-11-21 (406 ground truth words), on CPU only, as the LFM3-VL-2B experiments do, and are comparable to them. They are single runs without significance tests. CER and word accuracy are computed on the matched words only, so rows with different numbers of matches are not strictly comparable on those two columns.

    2026-11-21 — Indra as recogniser, as page reader and combined with the online detector

    • Hypothesis: Indra reads handwriting well enough to be useful in a pipeline, either behind a word detector or as a whole-page reader.
    • Setup: three spikes with throwaway scripts (not kept in the repository), indra-ocr 1.1.2:
      1. Crops: the YOLO detector cuts the words and each crop is read as one line box by Indra's recogniser, without its line detector (the 2026-10-09_yolo_detector_ppocrv5 pipeline with Indra in place of PP-OCRv5). Calling Indra's full pipeline on a crop instead made its detector split the word into overlapping fragments (CER about 130%) and is not reported.
      2. Page: the pipeline in this repository. Two box variants were compared: mid (boxes stretched to the midpoint of the gaps, the one implemented) and pad (boxes of the first to the last token plus 0.35 line heights).
      3. Combined with the online detector: the stroke-based detector supplies the word boxes and each takes the text of the Indra word that it overlaps most. Lines either from Indra's own detector or built from the stroke groups.
    • Command: uv run --extra indra python scripts/run_benchmark.py -p 2026-11-21_indra_page for the page pipeline (it reproduced its spike exactly).
    • Results:
    Variant Precision Recall CER (case-insens.) R×(1-CER) Word Acc
    Page, mid (the pipeline) 81.1% 74.9% 23.2% 57.5% 56.9% (173/304)
    Page, pad 78.4% 72.4% 22.8% 55.9% 57.1%
    Crops behind YOLO 77.0% 72.7% 42.4% 41.9% 38.6%
    Online groups + Indra, Indra's lines 84.1% 87.4% 37.0% 55.1% 46.5%
    Online groups + Indra, lines from groups 84.1% 87.4% 65.0% 30.6% 22.5%
    • Best model: page, mid.
    • Conclusion:
      • Indra needs the context of a line: on isolated word crops its CER is 42.4%, nearly double the 23.2% of page mode.
      • As a page reader its detector finds more words than YOLO does (precision 81.1% and recall 74.9% against 77.0% and 72.7%) and its CER is on a par with the best pipelines. The stroke-based online detector still finds far more (87.4% recall).
      • The wider mid boxes beat the tight pad boxes under the benchmark's strict 80% coverage match; the 1.6 point gap in R×(1-CER) is within what we would expect from noise.
      • Combining the online detector with Indra does not work: Indra's words and the stroke groups disagree on where words start and end, and assigning by overlap leaves 20 of 422 groups without text and gives many wrong ones. The variant with lines built from the stroke groups is broken (125 of 422 groups empty) for a reason that was not found: our line clustering is crude and the result says nothing about Indra.

    2026-11-21 — Comparison with the existing pipelines

    The pipelines from the LFM3-VL-2B comparison and Indra, sorted by R×(1-CER). The older rows were not rerun.

    Pipeline Precision Recall CER (case-insens.) R×(1-CER) Word Acc
    2026-11-03_online_word_detector_hub_simple_htr_ondb 84.1% 87.4% 27.3% 63.6% 53.8%
    2026-11-09_yolo_detector_lfm3_vl 77.0% 72.7% 19.6% 58.4% 66.4%
    2026-11-21_indra_page 81.1% 74.9% 23.2% 57.5% 56.9%
    2026-10-09_yolo_detector_ppocrv5 77.0% 72.7% 23.4% 55.7% 51.2%
    2026-10-17_yolo_detector_simple_htr_ondb_beam_vocab 77.0% 72.7% 24.6% 54.8% 60.3%
    2026-10-09_ppocrv5_det_rec 77.3% 51.2% 20.7% 40.6% 57.2%

    Indra is the second best pipeline without a server, and the only whole-page one that is competitive: PP-OCRv5's page mode finds far fewer words (recall 51.2%).
